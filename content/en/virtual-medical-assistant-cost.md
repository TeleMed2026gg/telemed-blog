Virtual medical assistant cost depends on what you're buying, not just the hourly rate. The real number includes supervision, training, technology, quality control and backup coverage, and vendors handle those very differently. To compare fairly, price the in-house role in full, then ask each vendor to show what its rate covers.

We don't publish rates, and any number you see online should be treated with care. Rates shift with location, language skills, scope and hours. What you can do is build a comparison that holds up.

## Start with the in-house benchmark

The [Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/medical-assistants.htm) reports a median wage of $21.97 per hour, or $45,690 per year, for medical assistants as of May 2025. For front desk and administrative roles, BLS puts the median for [medical secretaries and administrative assistants](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/office-and-administrative-support/secretaries-and-administrative-assistants.htm) at $45,930 per year.

Wages aren't the whole cost. BLS [employer compensation data for June 2026](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/ecec_09092026.htm) shows that for private-industry office and administrative support jobs, wages make up 68.7% of total compensation and benefits make up 31.3%.

Here's a rough calculation:

- Median medical assistant wage: $21.97 per hour
- Wages as a share of total compensation: 68.7%
- Estimated compensation: $21.97 ÷ 0.687 = about $32 per hour

That's an estimate, since it applies a broad office-occupation ratio to a health care wage. Your local pay and benefits will differ. BLS also projects 13% job growth for medical assistants from 2025 to 2035, so hiring pressure isn't likely to ease.

## The costs that don't show up on a pay stub

Add these to your in-house number. Use your own figures where you have them.

| Cost item | In-house | Ask the vendor |
|---|---|---|
| Wages and benefits | Your payroll | Is everything in the quoted rate? |
| Recruiting | Ads, agency fees, interview time | Who recruits and replaces staff? |
| Training | Onboarding hours from a lead | Is training billed? |
| Supervision | Manager time | Is a team lead included? |
| Absences | PTO and sick coverage | What happens when an assistant is out? |
| Space and equipment | Workstation, phone, software license | Who pays for equipment and licenses? |
| Turnover | Lost productivity, rehire cost | Is a replacement guaranteed, and how quickly? |
| Quality control | Manager audits | Are calls and work reviewed? |
| Compliance | HIPAA training, access reviews | What's the security program? |

For a worked version of this approach on the billing side, see [the real cost of an in-house medical biller](/blog/cost-of-in-house-medical-biller/).

## What drives virtual medical assistant pricing

Five things usually explain why two quotes for the "same" assistant look so different.

1. **Location and time zone.** Offshore, nearshore and U.S.-based staff are priced differently. Hours matter too. If your practice runs on Eastern or Central time, overnight shifts in Asia may be cheap on paper but costly in coordination.
2. **Skills and language.** A bilingual assistant with EHR experience is a different hire from a general admin. Verifying benefits and handling a denial take more training than sending reminders.
3. **Staffing model.** A dedicated assistant works for you alone and learns your templates and providers. A shared pool can cost less, but your patients may reach someone new each time.
4. **Scope.** Calls, scheduling, intake, verification and prior auth need different skill levels. Vague scope creates extra charges later.
5. **Volume and hours.** Part-time, after-hours and weekend coverage may be priced differently. Ask about minimum hours.

## Tasks that fit a virtual medical assistant

Clear administrative work fits best. Hands-on care doesn't.

| Good fit | Handle with care | Keep on-site |
|---|---|---|
| Scheduling and reminders | Refill requests under your protocol | Rooming and vitals |
| Inbound and outbound calls | Message triage with clinical escalation | Injections and specimen collection |
| Insurance verification | Prior authorization prep | Anything requiring clinical judgment |
| Referral coordination | Inbox management | In-person patient support |
| Waitlist and no-show recovery | Chart prep | |

Telemed isn't a telemedicine company, and virtual assistants in this model don't provide care. Anything touching clinical decisions needs a licensed person and a written protocol.

## Red flags in a quote

Watch for these when you read a proposal:

- A low rate with extra charges for setup, licenses, supervision or reporting
- No named backup when your assistant is out
- A long contract with no pilot period or way out
- No clear answer on where patient data sits or how access is controlled
- A marketplace or freelancer arrangement where no one owns quality
- No Business Associate Agreement for work involving protected health information
- Promises of guaranteed savings or results

If you're looking at offshore options, also check state rules on where records can be stored. Our guide to [Florida's offshore PHI law](/blog/florida-offshore-phi-law-408-051/) explains one example.

## Measure value, not just cost

A cheaper assistant isn't a better deal if calls still go unanswered. Compare what the role produces, too:

- Calls answered and abandoned
- Appointments booked or recovered per week
- Referrals completed
- Verified visits before the date of service
- Staff hours returned to in-office work

In our experience, systematically returning missed calls in off-peak hours can recover up to about 10% more appointments, and calling no-shows to reschedule can recover up to about 40% of those visits. Your results depend on your schedule and patient mix, so measure from your own baseline. Our guide on [missed calls in a medical practice](/blog/missed-calls-medical-practice/) shows how.

## When a virtual medical assistant isn't the right call

Skip it, or wait, if:

- Most of the work is hands-on and in the office.
- Your workflows live in people's heads and nobody has documented them. Fix that first.
- Your providers don't want to delegate messages or calendars.
- You don't have someone who can manage the relationship for an hour or two each week.
- You need one person a few hours a month. A part-time local hire may be simpler.

## How to test the cost before committing

Run a short pilot on one job. A 30-day trial focused on confirmations and rescheduling gives you hard numbers: the cost of the service against the appointments it recovered. At Telemed, this is how we often start, with [dedicated bilingual teams](https://telemed.com.co/us/#services) working in the same time zones as U.S. practices. Measure it in dollars, and decide from there.

## Key takeaways

- The quoted rate isn't the total cost. Ask what's included.
- A median-wage U.S. medical assistant costs roughly $32 per hour with benefits, before recruiting, training and supervision.
- Location, skills, staffing model and scope drive most price differences.
- Virtual assistants handle administrative work, not clinical care.
- Compare output as well as cost, and test with a short pilot.
