Offshore billing, usually in India or the Philippines, wins on hourly rate. Nearshore billing, usually in Latin America, wins on time-zone overlap, live communication with your staff and payers, and Spanish-language patient work. For most small and mid-size US practices the deciding factors are less about price per hour and more about rework, response time, and how much supervision the arrangement needs from you. This guide compares both models honestly and gives you a checklist for choosing.

## Nearshore vs. offshore at a glance

| Factor | Nearshore (Latin America) | Offshore (Asia) |
|---|---|---|
| Hourly rate | Moderate | Lowest |
| Working-hour overlap with US clinics | Full: same or adjacent time zones | Partial: often overnight shifts |
| Live calls to payers during US hours | Native schedule | Requires night shifts |
| Spanish-speaking patients | Native fluency | Rarely available |
| Real-time questions from your staff | Same day, often same hour | Usually next business day |
| Travel to visit the team | A few hours from Miami, Houston or Dallas | Long-haul |
| Contracting | Varies; some vendors contract through a US entity | Varies |

Neither column is "right". The right model depends on what work you're sending.

## Where offshore works well

Offshore teams have run large-scale billing operations for decades. They work well for **high-volume, well-documented, asynchronous work**:

- Charge entry from clean documentation
- Payment posting from ERAs
- Bulk coding with clear guidelines and a strong QA layer
- Overnight work queues that are ready when your office opens

If your process is stable, documented and doesn't need back-and-forth during your business day, the lower hourly rate can be a real advantage.

## Where nearshore pulls ahead

Revenue cycle work isn't all asynchronous. The parts that leak the most money tend to need **live interaction during US hours**:

- **Payer phone calls.** A/R follow-up, denial clarification and authorization status checks happen on payer lines that keep US business hours.
- **Questions to your staff.** "Which modifier did Dr. Lee intend?" is a five-minute fix at 10 a.m. and a lost day if it waits for an overnight email chain.
- **Patient balances and billing questions.** Many US patients prefer Spanish. The U.S. Census Bureau reports that Spanish is by far the most common language other than English spoken at home ([Census language use data](https://www.census.gov/topics/population/language-use.html)).
- **Front-office and back-office together.** Eligibility checks, scheduling, prior authorizations and billing affect one another. Teams on the same clock as your front desk catch problems before they become denials.

In our experience, the hidden cost in any outsourcing model is **rework and supervision time**: the hours your office manager spends clarifying, correcting and chasing. A lower hourly rate that needs twice the supervision isn't cheaper.

## Compliance: same rules, different questions

HIPAA applies the same way wherever your vendor sits. Any vendor handling protected health information (PHI) must sign a **Business Associate Agreement (BAA)** and apply appropriate safeguards. The questions to ask are practical:

1. **Where is the data stored?** Ideally it stays in your US-hosted EHR and PM system, not copied to vendor servers.
2. **Who can access what?** Individual logins, role-based access, the minimum-necessary principle and access logs you can review.
3. **How is the workstation secured?** Locked-down devices, no local downloads, no personal phones on the floor, encrypted connections.
4. **What about state law?** Florida requires certain licensed providers to keep patient information physically stored in the US, its territories or Canada ([Florida § 408.051](https://www.polsinelli.com/publications/florida-imposes-a-ban-on-offshoring-storage-of-medical-information)). Texas SB 1188 adds a similar US storage requirement, effective January 1, 2026 ([Hall Render summary](https://hallrender.com/2025/08/19/new-texas-law-on-ehr-data-localization-and-ai/)). These laws focus on where records are **stored**, so a team working inside your US-hosted systems is a different situation from a vendor copying data abroad. Confirm the details with your counsel.

Some vendors go further and design workflows that limit PHI exposure, for example AI voice agents that handle confirmations without a human seeing the record, or tokenized identifiers so agents work a queue without seeing full patient details. Ask what options exist for your highest-risk workflows.

## Total cost: what to actually compare

Don't compare hourly rates. Compare **cost per outcome** over 90 days:

- Clean claim rate before and after
- Days in A/R and the share of A/R over 90 days
- Denial rate and the share of denials appealed
- Hours your own staff spent supervising or fixing vendor work
- Collections per hour of vendor time

A pilot that measures these numbers is worth more than any sales deck, including ours.

## A checklist for choosing your billing partner

- **Dedicated or shared team?** Dedicated agents learn your providers, payers and quirks. Shared pools rarely do.
- **Inside your systems?** The work and the audit trail should live in your EHR and PM system.
- **US contracting?** A US entity and US-law contract simplify disputes and BAAs.
- **Hours of overlap?** Get specific: which hours will someone answer when your staff calls?
- **Reporting?** Monthly KPIs by payer, not just "hours worked".
- **Exit terms?** Reasonable notice periods and a documented handover if you leave.
- **Pilot?** A defined 30- to 90-day pilot with success metrics agreed up front.

## Key takeaways

- Offshore is strong for high-volume, well-documented, overnight work. Nearshore is strong for work that needs live interaction during US hours.
- Rework and supervision time decide real cost more than hourly rate does.
- HIPAA obligations are identical in both models. Ask about data storage, access control and, in Florida and Texas, state storage rules.
- Choose based on a measured pilot, not a price sheet.
