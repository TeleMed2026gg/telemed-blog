Missed calls at a medical practice are missed revenue, and most practices don't know how much. The fix starts with numbers: pull 30 days of call data, find out how many calls go unanswered and when, and estimate how many were patients trying to book. Then put a same-day callback process in place and adjust coverage to match when patients actually call.

## Why the phone line deserves a closer look

Phone access is back on practice leaders' agendas. In a [December 2025 MGMA Stat poll](https://www.mgma.com/mgma-stat/patient-access-priorities-for-2026), 22% of medical groups named phone access as their top patient-access priority for 2026, close behind no-shows and online scheduling.

The line is also crowded with work that isn't booking. In a [March 2026 MGMA Stat poll](https://www.mgma.com/mgma-stat/phones-are-still-a-backlog-costing-medical-practices-time), leaders said eligibility and prior authorization calls take the most staff phone time (45%), followed by scheduling (31%). When the same people handle payer calls and patient calls, patients wait.

And patients already wait long enough for care. [AMN Healthcare's 2025 survey](https://www.techtarget.com/patientengagement/news/366624919/Average-patient-appointment-wait-time-is-31-days-in-2025) found an average wait of 31 days for a new-patient physician appointment across 15 large metro areas, up 19% since 2022. A patient who can't get through may simply try the next practice on the list.

## Step 1: Pull the data

Ask your phone provider or VoIP admin panel for a 30-day report. You want, by hour and by day of week:

- Total inbound calls
- Answered calls
- Abandoned calls (caller hung up while waiting)
- Calls that went to voicemail
- Calls received before opening and after closing
- Average hold time before answer or abandonment
- Calls by IVR option selected, if you have a phone tree

If your system can't produce this, that's your first finding. You can't manage a line you can't measure.

## Step 2: Estimate the revenue at stake

Use a simple worksheet. Every input should come from your own data. The numbers below are an **illustration**, not a benchmark.

| Input | How to get it | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Missed calls per month (abandoned + voicemail + after-hours) | Phone report | 600 |
| Share of missed calls that were about scheduling | Sample 50 voicemails or callbacks | 40% |
| Share of those callers who never call back or book | Match callback attempts to the schedule | 30% |
| Lost appointments per month | 600 × 40% × 30% | 72 |
| Average revenue per new appointment, including follow-ups | Billing report by visit type | Your number |
| **Revenue at risk per month** | Lost appointments × average revenue | **72 × your number** |

Two cautions. Don't count repeat calls from the same patient twice; dedupe by phone number. And new-patient calls are worth more than routine ones because they lead to follow-up visits, so separate them if you can.

## Step 3: Find where calls are lost

Missed calls cluster. In our operations, we usually find them in a few places:

- **Peak hours.** Monday mornings and the first hour after lunch often get more calls than staff can answer.
- **Just before opening and just after closing.** Patients call on their way to work or after it. Compare call attempts with your posted hours. Shifting phone coverage by an hour can capture calls you miss every day, without adding headcount.
- **The phone tree.** Long IVR menus, options that loop back, and paths that end in a general voicemail all push callers to hang up. Walk every path yourself.
- **Voicemail boxes nobody owns.** Messages routed to medical assistants often wait until the end of a clinic day, or longer. Each voicemail queue needs an owner and a same-day standard.
- **Mixed workloads.** When the person answering the phone is also checking in patients and calling payers, the phone loses.

## Step 4: Fix the line

Start with the fixes that cost the least and recover the most.

1. **Same-day callbacks.** Call back every missed call and voicemail during the quieter part of the day. In our experience, returning missed calls systematically recovers up to about 10% more appointments. It's usually the fastest win.
2. **Redesign the IVR.** Put scheduling first, keep the menu to three or four options, offer Spanish if your patients need it, and make sure every path ends with a person or a booked appointment.
3. **Match coverage to demand.** Use the hourly report to staff peak hours and the edges of the day.
4. **Separate the work.** Move payer calls, eligibility checks and prior authorizations away from the people answering patient lines.
5. **Cover after hours.** Decide whether after-hours calls go to an answering service, an AI agent that can book simple appointments, or a callback queue for the next morning. Any of these beats a full voicemail box.
6. **Automate the simple calls carefully.** AI voice agents can handle confirmations, basic scheduling and FAQs and transfer complex calls to staff. Start with low-risk call types and review transcripts weekly.

## Step 5: Measure every month

| Metric | Target direction |
|---|---|
| Abandonment rate | Down |
| Voicemails returned same day | Up, toward 100% |
| Average speed of answer | Down |
| Appointments booked from callbacks | Track and report |
| After-hours calls handled | Up |
| Revenue from recovered appointments | Report in dollars |

Report recovered revenue next to the cost of the fix. That's the number that tells you whether a change paid for itself. It also connects to your no-show work, since the same callback team can recover missed appointments. ## When outsourcing the phone line doesn't make sense

If your practice gets a modest number of calls, has one experienced front-desk person who knows every patient, and rarely misses calls, keep it in-house and just tighten the callback process. Outsourcing makes more sense when volume swings, when the front desk is also handling payers, when you need bilingual coverage, or when after-hours calls go unanswered.

## Key takeaways

- Pull 30 days of call data before changing anything.
- Estimate revenue at risk with your own numbers: missed calls, share that were scheduling, share that never rebook, and revenue per appointment.
- Same-day callbacks are the fastest fix.
- Audit your IVR, voicemail ownership and phone-line hours.
- Separate payer work from patient calls, and report results in dollars.
